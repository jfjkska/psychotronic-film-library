Milano Calibro 9 (1972) is an Italian poliziottesco thriller written and
directed by Fernando Di Leo. The cast includes Gastone Moschin as Ugo Piazza,
just released from prison and pursued by the police and his old gangland
buddies for a missing $300,000. He is soon back working with crime boss The
Mikado, played by Lionel Stander (Max from 80s TV staple Hart to Hart). Ugo
hooks up with old girlfriend and go-go dancer Nelly Gordon, played by Barbara
Bouchet, who dances in a bejewelled costume described by the police
commissioner as "dangling dingleberries".

Things start to unravel for the crime gang, with explosions and assassinations
in public toilets, tit-for-tat shootouts and double-crossings across Milan.
The soundtrack is provided by Luis Bacalov and prog rockers Osanna. It veers
from lounge lushness and hard-boiled jazz to crazy freak-flute tribal rock
wig-outs. Nicely photographed in Milan, Di Leo makes good use of the city
locations. A totally satisfying entry into the genre, and it ticks all the
boxes for an Italian crime thriller.
