Turkey Shoot (1982), aka Blood Camp Thatcher, is a seminal Ozploitation
action thriller directed by Brian Trenchard-Smith. A convicted freedom
fighter played by Steve Railsback (Lifeforce) and an innocent prisoner
played by Olivia Hussey (Black Christmas) are sent to re-education Camp 47
for "deviants". They try to survive the sadistic camp master Charles
Thatcher (Michael Craig) and his head guard Ritter (Roger Ward).

The camp motto is:

> Freedom is obedience, obedience is work, work is life.

Anybody who disobeys is met with extreme cruelty, and punishments are
lethal. Thatcher organises a game of hunting the "deviants" for rich and
influential clients. One of the clients brings along a cannibalistic
troglodyte called Alph. I am sure it made sense when they wrote it.

Brian May, not the one from Queen, provides a score of sometimes jaunty,
bouncy synth, staccato stabs and booming percussion, which fits the pace
of the film when the hunt is under way.

Intended as a satire on corporate fascism, it has earned its place as one
of the most notorious films in the Ozploitation canon. Critics described
it as "a vomitous offering" and "a catalogue of sickening horrors".

It was released in UK cinemas with 1 minute and 18 seconds of compulsory
cuts made by the BBFC to secure an 18 rating. When submitted for video
release in 1993, it faced further cuts of 5 minutes and 41 seconds. Only
in 2003 was the film released in its entirety.

For fans of bloody dystopian dramas where good taste is left at the
camp gate.
