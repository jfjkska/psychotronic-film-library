Hannah, Queen of the Vampires, aka Crypt of the Living Dead, does not fulfil
its bloody opening. The Spanish/American production from 1973 ultimately lacks
bite. It shuffles along for most of the film; Hannah doesn't make much of a
presence until two thirds in, when she finally starts to terrorise the
islanders.

The soundtrack by Philip Lambro is probably the best thing about it. The
direction by Julio Salvador is pedestrian at best. The cinematography makes
the best of the Mediterranean landscape but is not enough to lift it from the
depths of the undead. Teresa Gimpera has an ethereal quality as Hannah but is
easily despatched by leading man Andrew Prine.

The Wild Man of the island is never explained. He is in from the beginning
without any concept of where he came from and how he got involved in serving
the vampire queen. The twist with the children feels tacked on and has been
done better in the first Count Yorga film.

It made me yearn for the gothic horror of Hammer; at least they knew how to
build the drama. Another Euro curiosity, which will probably get a release
through Severin Films.
