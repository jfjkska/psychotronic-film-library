Schizo (1976) is Pete Walker and long-time writing partner David
McGillivray's sojourn into psychodrama. The film begins with narration:
"Schizophrenia, a mental disorder. Sometimes known as multiple or split
personality. Characterised by loss of touch within an environment, an
alternation between violent and contrasting behaviour patterns."

It sets the scene for Lynne Frederick as Samantha, being stalked on her
wedding day by a man from her past. Bloody mayhem ensues, with Walker taking a
sledgehammer to any nuance. Heads are bludgeoned, throats are cut, and even a
knitting needle proves a bloody end for British TV stalwart Queenie Watts. The
interior decor of shag pile carpet and heavy patterned wallpaper is a 1970s
wonder in itself.

Walker is a fine director and keeps the camera moving to keep the identity of
the killer hidden, although we all know who it really is all along. Walker
must have a clause in his contract that all his leading ladies get naked at
some point. This is true of Lynne Frederick, as it was for Susan Penhaligon in
House of Mortal Sin and Vicki Michelle in House of Whipcord.

You know what you are getting with a Pete Walker (Heritage) production: lots
of location shoots and lounge and bedroom interiors, a sizeable body count,
and a cast that is familiar to anyone growing up in 1970s Britain.
