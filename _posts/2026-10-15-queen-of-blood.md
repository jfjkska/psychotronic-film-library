Queen of Blood (1966) was written and directed by Curtis Harrington. This
sci-fi horror is set in 1990, starring John Saxon, Basil Rathbone, Dennis
Hopper, Judi Meredith and Florence Marly as the Alien Queen.

Aliens make contact and announce their imminent visit to Earth. Their
ship crashes on Mars and two spaceships are sent to investigate. On board
the first ship are Laura (Meredith) and Paul (Hopper). The second spaceship
contains Allan (Saxon), who is in a relationship with Laura. On Earth,
coordinating the rescue is Doctor Farraday (Rathbone).

The team find a female alien, and it's not long before she is feeding on
the crew to sustain her appetite for blood. The crew try to appease her
with their stores of plasma, but her appetite is insatiable and the plasma
is not enough. She feasts on Allan and, in a scuffle with Laura, she is
injured and bleeds to death. Allan surmises that she must be the queen of
her race and part of a haemophilic bloodline sent to breed with humans.

With only Laura and Allan left, they return to Earth and discover the
queen has laid pulsing, glowing eggs around the ship. Allan wants the eggs
destroyed, but Dr Farraday overrules him to save the eggs for research.

An alien killing the crew of a spaceship, pulsing, glowing eggs. Does
this sound familiar? Dan O'Bannon has cited Queen of Blood as a major
visual and conceptual influence on writing the screenplay for Alien
(1979). Harrington said that Alien "was a greatly enhanced, expensive and
elaborate version of Queen of Blood".

It is not the most visually exciting film; the effects are a bit shoddy,
it is slow in parts and the Alien Queen is underused. With that in mind,
it is an interesting chapter in the development of cosmic horror.
