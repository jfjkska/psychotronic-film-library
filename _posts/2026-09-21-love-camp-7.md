Love Camp 7 (1969) was reportedly the first in the notorious Naziploitation
genre of films. Directed by Lee Frost, and with a cast that includes
exploitation kingpin David F. Friedman, you know the bar is going to be set
low.

The plot is basically two Women's Army Corps volunteers going undercover in
the Love Camp to serve as sex slaves to the visiting German officers. Their
mission is to rescue a Jewish scientist who is interned in the camp and has
secrets to help the allies. What follows is a catalogue of humiliation,
torture, sexual abuse and some very questionable German accents. Some of the
cast do not even attempt to sound German and speak in an American drawl that
wouldn't sound out of place in a Western. It builds to a brutal climax in
which all the German officers and some of the prisoners end up in a bloody
heap before the escape to freedom.

It was one of the video nasties banned in the UK and was rejected for
certification again in 2020. It remains unclassified by the British Board of
Film Classification today.

Ilsa, She Wolf of the SS had the advantage of using the set of Hogan's Heroes,
and probably a bit more cash from David F. Friedman as producer, but as the
film that kickstarted a genre, Love Camp 7 is a particularly important chapter
in grindhouse, sleaze and video exploitation cinema.
