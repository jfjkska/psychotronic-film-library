The Black Room (1982) is an American horror, written by Norman Thaddeus
Vane and co-directed with Elly Kenner. It concerns Larry, a husband and
father who rents out a room in the Hollywood Hills for his secret
liaisons with a college student and a prostitute. Unknown to him, the
owners, siblings Bridget and Jason, are spying through a two-way mirror
and photographing Larry and friends at play. They are also draining the
blood of their tenants to keep Jason alive. Bridget reveals to Larry that
Jason has a rare blood condition which requires a transfusion every two
weeks. Larry's wife Robin finds out about his affairs and decides two can
play that game, teaming up with the ghoulish siblings to use the black
room for her own needs.

There are hypodermic needles over a foot long, a bath full of blood, and
plenty of transfusing going on. You would think with all this voyeurism,
blood-letting and body-burying that this would be a great recipe for a
tense horror. Unfortunately, Cassandra Gava as Bridget and Stephen Knight
as Jason seem to have graduated from the timber yard of acting, such is
their lack of screen presence. There is an early appearance from future
scream queen Linnea Quigley as babysitter Milly, who is nearly bled to
death by Jason before being rescued by the now-redemptive Larry.

The electronic soundtrack is by James Ackley and Art Podell. It's the
usual 80s electronic score, nothing exceptional, but it creaks and
squeaks when necessary. Ackley and Podell do throw in the kitchen sink of
heavy sonic synth in the final few minutes, when the film goes full
phantasmagoria. The finale sees both Bridget and Jason rise from the
dead, covered in buckets of the red stuff, continuing their pursuit of
Larry and his family. Robin wonders aloud if "people like that ever really
die?" Who knows, and frankly who really cares.

It's not a surprise to learn Stephen Knight didn't do much with his
wooden acting skills after The Black Room. Cassandra Gava had a career
before it, and was cover star of Jefferson Starship's Spitfire album,
credited as the "Dragon Princess." A poster of the album features in the
film, with Gava riding a dragon; it's meant to be one of Jason's many
photos of Bridget.

Linnea Quigley went on to appear in many exploitation films, including
Creepozoids (1987), Hollywood Chainsaw Hookers (1987) and Night of the
Demons (1989). She even rode the crest of the home aerobics wave with
Linnea Quigley's Horror Workout (1990). In my opinion, The Black Room is
best enjoyed as a side dish, served with a more satisfying horror main
from the considerable list of films of that year.
