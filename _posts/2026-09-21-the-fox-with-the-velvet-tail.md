The Fox with the Velvet Tail (1971) is a giallo in style, but without the
usual stalk and slash, and with lashings of twists and turns. Directed by Jose
Maria Forque.

A Spanish/Italian production, it has a sharp visual eye, and the eye features
from the first frame and is artfully used to emphasise the tension. The
nefarious affairs play out at the sun-drenched villa as the players plot and
execute their plans.

Soft-focus eroticism adds rather than distracts, although the love scene with
Jean Sorel hanging upside down and topless from a tree is certainly a first
for me.

With only one person killed in the entire film, this is more psychological
than brutal, but is a decent addition to the list of Euro thrillers under my
viewing belt.
