Burial Ground, aka Nights of Terror (1981), is an Italian zombie exploitation
horror directed by Andrea Bianchi. An archeology professor discovers an
ancient crypt and unleashes a horde of the undead. A group of people arrive at
the professor's home, invited to celebrate his discovery. Little do they know
the professor is now one of the zombies, and they are coming for them. And
come they do, from under sods of earth, from bushes and ornamental planters of
all things.

The zombies are appropriately decayed, with maggot-infested faces, crusty eye
sockets and buck teeth. The film was made in just four weeks and the majority
of the budget went on make-up and effects. Well done to Gino De Rossi and
Rosario Prestopino for the make-up and effects. The zombies may be slow, but
they ooze muddy liquid when shot and their heads crumble like cookies. The
gore is plentiful, and there are wheelbarrow loads of offal to go around for
the hungry dead.

The cast includes Peter Bark (Pietro Barzocchini), who at 25 years old is one
of the oddest choices for an adult playing a child I have watched. The scene
when Michael munches down on his mother's breast is a sight to behold. Poor
Mariangela Giordano was just happy to have him close after finding him
armless on the bathroom floor.

The film score begins with a be-bop jazz vibe but soon morphs into space rock
sonic warbles when the zombies take hold. Criticised as sacrificing plot for
creative death scenes, the zombies utilise a scythe, axes and a battering ram
to get their fill. The gore does a lot of carrying, as the pacing is uneven.
There is a laissez-faire attitude to one of the group meeting a grisly end,
and the film concludes with the last two of the group facing a bloody end.

The film leaves us with the following quote from the "Prophecy of the Black
Spider":

> The earth shall tremble...  
> graves shall open...  
> they shall come among the living  
> as messengers of death there shall be  
> the nights of terror...

You have been warned.

Director Andrea Bianchi was prolific in exploitation cinema and has [Strip
Nude for Your Killer]({{ '/reviews/strip-nude-for-your-killer/' | relative_url }})
(1975) among his directing credits. This can also be found on the shelves of
the Psychotronic Film Library.
