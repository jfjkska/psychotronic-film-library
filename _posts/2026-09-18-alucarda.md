Alucarda is a 1977 Mexican occult horror which loosely falls within the
nunsploitation genre. There is enough hysterical hyperbole within and outside
the convent walls, including flogging, nudity and satanic recitations, to tip
this into pure exploitation territory.

Directed by Juan López Moctezuma, the plot centres on newly orphaned teenager
Justine, who is befriended by the enigmatic Alucarda, played by a spirited Tina
Romero. Alucarda apparently means Daughter of Darkness. What follows is a
rollercoaster of occult possession, human combustion and a fair amount of
bloodletting.

It was derided in its native Mexico, described by Mexican film critic Leonardo
García Tsao as "one of the ten worst films he had ever seen". It won more favour
outside Mexico, with Michael Weldon of the Psychotronic Video Guide writing that
it was "the strongest, most imaginative and visual witch movie since Ken
Russell's The Devils". Carlos Aguilar praised Romero's performance: "a raw
portrayal of a mind ravaged by a diabolical energy hits with hair-raising
strength".

Certainly for me this was a fortuitous find that sits among the more
interesting world exploitation cinema offerings. At 77 minutes it packs a lot
into its compact running time. A recommendation to seek out if you are a fan of
occult horror.
