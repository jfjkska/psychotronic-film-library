The Devil's Plaything (1973) is a eurosleaze vampire film from Joseph W.
Sarno. It's as if Sarno had watched Rollin and Franco and decided their
films weren't filled with enough sexy vampires for his liking, and turned
it up to 11. There is groovy naked dancing to a hypnotic bongo beat that
casts a spell over the uninitiated. Housekeeper and high priestess Wanda
wanders the castle with dark eyes and flaring nostrils.

There is a plot about descendants of a vampire, Baroness Varga, burnt at
the stake for her unholy goings-on. This is all secondary, though, and
soon strangers Helga and Peter are literally rolling in the hay, and
Monika is falling under the spell of Baroness Varga's portrait. Julia, an
anthropologist who has studied local superstitions, clutches her crucifix
of cloves in a bid to keep from falling under the power of the
vampyric-worshipping servants of evil.

"I believe the spirit of Baroness Varga walks this corridor," Julia says
to Helga, who seems unable to satiate her own burning desires. Ulrike Butz
as Monika goes from innocence to darkly captivating when fully possessed
by Baroness Varga; with her black hair and red lips, she feasts on the
spellbound victims.

The Devil's Plaything is a notable contemporary of Rollin's early-70s
films, and sits comfortably alongside Jess Franco's Vampyros Lesbos
(1971) and José Ramón Larraz's Vampyres (1975) in the erotic vampire
canon.
