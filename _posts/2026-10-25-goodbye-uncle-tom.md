Goodbye Uncle Tom, aka Addio Zio Tom (1971), is a mondo docudrama produced and directed by Gualtiero Jacopetti and Franco Prosperi. The film begins in modern America in the swathe of political corruption, assassinations and the call for armed revolution from the Black Panthers. A white middle class woman with a black chauffeur narrates how her family had five hundred slaves to maintain the house. This isn't Driving Miss Daisy but a lament to the hierarchy of white owners and black people who work without pay and are victim to their owners' whims and fancies.

The filmmakers view this modern context of oppression and prejudice and travel back in time in a helicopter to document the Antebellum South. The film is based on published documents and materials to provide the viewpoint of the white slave traders and owners. The following historical document is a relentless treatise of inhumane cruelty, degradation and exploitation of a captive people. I don't have to list the torturous behaviours that befall the slaves, but be warned: if you do watch this film, it is a grim experience that stays with you. Goodbye Uncle Tom shows the complicity of state institutions that controlled the treatment of slaves. As one clergyman preaches to his congregation:

"God decreed by law that slavery will be honoured and obeyed, being his ordainment. Slavery is therefore a divine institution."

The filmmakers chose to shoot the majority of it in Haiti with the support of cruel dictator Papa Doc Duvalier. He supplied hundreds of impoverished Haitians to act as extras and undergo the exploitation that their ancestors would have experienced. An irony not lost on critics who labelled the filmmakers racist in their intent.

The third act deals with a young black man reading William Styron's novel The Confessions of Nat Turner and fantasizing about bloody retribution on the middle class white people he sees around him. This is depicted as an elderly couple attacked with hatchets in their bed, a family bloodily cut down and someone being drowned in the sea. The bloody mayhem happens while fuzzed-out rock plays in the background, adding to the intensity.

The soundtrack by Riz Ortolani is a mix of romantic sweeps, oompah marching band and the fuzzy freak rock score. The opening song, Oh My Love, sung by Katyna Ranieri, has a sweetness that sits uncomfortably with the grim cruelty that is about to unfold.

The film was cut by thirty minutes by the British Board of Film Censors, mainly for its depictions of torture and sexual assault. Film critic Roger Ebert described it thus: "they made the most disgusting, contemptuous insult to decency ever to masquerade as a documentary". This is coming from the man who wrote Beneath the Valley of the Ultra-Vixens (1979).

Goodbye Uncle Tom is not recommended viewing but serves as an important document on the mondo documentary that made Jacopetti and Prosperi the leaders in this particular form of exploitation cinema.
