The House That Screamed, aka La residencia (1969), is a Spanish gothic
slasher. Directed by Narciso Ibáñez Serrador, it features Lilli Palmer as
Señora Fourneau and John Moulder-Brown as the Oedipal Luis.

Señora Fourneau is the strict headmistress of a secluded boarding school
for wayward girls in 19th-century France. She literally cracks the whip
when one of the students steps out of line, and discipline is enforced by
her protégée, Mademoiselle Tupan. Three students have already gone
missing by the time Teresa arrives at the school. She develops a
relationship with the repressed Luis, and the two are soon meeting for
secret liaisons in the boiler room. The numbers are bumped up to five, in
brutal fashion.

The macabre reveal is effective, and the relationship between Luis and
his domineering mother literally comes to a head. Described as "a
thoroughly convincing, even elegant exercise in evil — all this depravity
has been served up with considerable skill," The House That Screamed has
been hailed as an early progenitor of the slasher, and influential on the
European giallo films that followed.
