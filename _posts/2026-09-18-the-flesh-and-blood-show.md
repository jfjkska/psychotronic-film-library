More Pete Walker early-70s fare with The Flesh and Blood Show. Ray Brooks
(House of Whipcord) and Robin Askwith ([Cool It Carol!]({{ '/reviews/cool-it-carol/' | relative_url }}))
are among a group of unemployed actors lured to an abandoned theatre in an
out-of-season seaside town. Promised weeks of rehearsals before taking their
improvised production to London, they begin rehearsing in earnest in fur
bikinis and whatever they can find in the abandoned props department.

Bloody chaos ensues as actors disappear and are replaced to make up the
numbers. One actor is decapitated by a prop guillotine, another stands naked
with a dagger in her back. Yes, it's improbable and none of it makes real
sense. Something akin to an Agatha Christie whodunnit with nudity, The Flesh
and Blood Show was originally released with a 3D scene, which is now black and
white in its non-cinema version.

Pete Walker is reliable for several things: location shooting, a cast of
British thesps willing to slum it for a few weeks, and an almost comedic
approach to his slashers. Walker can be relied upon to get his actresses to
undress, or in the case of Jenny Hanley a body double will suffice, so as not
to ruin her future career in British children's television.
