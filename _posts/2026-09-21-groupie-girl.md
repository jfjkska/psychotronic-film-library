Groupie Girl (1970), directed by British exploitation kingpin Derek Ford, is a
bleak, seamy peek into the music scene, co-written by former groupie Suzanne
Mercer. The film follows Sally, played by Esme Johns, who stows away with a
touring band. Sally is bored of her small town existence and is looking for an
escape to London. What she encounters is being a commodity to be passed around
to other touring bands when she outstays her welcome.

Resolutely downbeat in its portrayal, it is the darker cousin to films such as
Herman's Hermits' star vehicle, Mrs Brown, You've Got a Lovely Daughter
(1968). Notable British actors Donald Sumpter, James Beck and Ken Hutchison
(Straw Dogs) all feature, alongside members of Brit psych band Opal Butterfly.
The worthy inclusion of Mary and Madeleine Collinson (Twins of Evil) adds
glamour to the grimy array of discarded joints and coffee stains that colour
its palette.
