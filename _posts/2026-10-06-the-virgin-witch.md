The Virgin Witch (1971) is an occult exploitation classic directed by Ray
Austin. Described by critics as an "amusingly dated British horror
cum-skin flick" and "an entertaining and occasionally sleazy slice of
seventies occult-themed picture with some great style and memorable
scenes."

I could stop there, but there is much to discuss. Starring real-life
sisters Vicki and Ann Michelle as Betty and Christine, the film opens with
the sisters absconding from their small-town existence and heading to
London to seek their fortune. They are picked up by Johnny, who provides
lodgings with his mum. He soon strikes up a relationship with the
innocent Betty, while Christine looks for modelling vacancies.

It is quickly made apparent that Christine has a sixth sense, which
reveals a magazine advert for "the wizard of modelling," Miss Sybil Waite
(Patricia Haines). Sybil has an eye for gorgeous women, and Christine is
no exception. In the drop of a witch's hat, the sisters are joining Sybil
at Wychwold Hall. "The witches used to dance here. Some people say they
still do," says Sybil, as she strolls the grounds. In an instant,
Christine is being photographed running, jumping, and — with the
encouragement of photographer Peter — getting naked and clutching an
apple (the photo shoot is supposedly for a cider brand).

It is revealed to the sisters that Wychwold is home to a coven, with
Sybil as High Priestess and Doctor of Literature Gerald as High Priest.
Gerald recognises Christine's special power, and she is soon prepared for
initiation into the coven. Cue naked dancing, altar intercourse and wild
music as the coven builds to its climax. Christine's powers grow, and a
power struggle ensues with High Priestess Sybil, who is targeted into
submission by the more powerful usurper.

Meanwhile, Betty's erstwhile boyfriend Johnny comes searching for her. He
agrees to Christine's demands to take part in a ceremony to initiate
Betty, so he can rescue her from all the naked witches. Betty is saved
but is innocent no more. Christine's fate is sealed when he fatally
despatches Sybil through her use of witchcraft.

I must give a special mention to Gerald Moss for his cinematography,
which is beyond expectations for an exploitation film. The soundtrack by
Ted Dicks uses piano, Hammond organ and an array of percussion to set the
mood for each scene. Filmed at the same location as Norman J. Warren's
occult classic [Satan's Slave](/reviews/satans-slave/), The Virgin Witch
would make a great double bill with the former.

The film was released in 1971, but due to censorship issues it only had a
limited release in London. It would be 1972 before it was released
nationwide, with several cuts to appease the British Board of Censors. It
is now available in its entirety.

The Michelle sisters have both since disowned the film. Vicki went on to
British TV sitcom fame in 'Allo 'Allo!; Ann appeared in future
exploitation classics Psychomania (1973) and House of Whipcord (1974).
There is even a novelisation by Klaus Vogel, published by Corgi, but it
fetches a high price in the paperback collectors' market. I will have to
stick with the film for now.
