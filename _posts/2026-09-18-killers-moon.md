What film has LSD-induced, droog-like maniacs escaped from a mental institution
on a murderous rampage believing they are in a collective dream, a hotel of
stranded schoolgirls and a three-legged vengeful dog? Well, that is what
Killer's Moon delivers in spades.

By 1978 Alan Birkinshaw had moved on from TV commercials and sexploitation
films. He was looking to take on Pete Walker and Norman J. Warren on the sleazy
horror front, and with Killer's Moon he provides something that sets it apart
among his contemporaries. Once described as "the most tasteless film in British
cinema history", the plot has a group of stranded schoolgirls and teachers
taking refuge in an out-of-season hotel in the Lake District. Meanwhile four
escaped homicidal patients are encroaching on the hotel, leaving corpses and
mutilated animals in their wake, one such animal being the three-legged dog.
Under the illusion that they are in a collective dream state, by use of LSD and
dream therapy, they believe they can do anything without consequence.

The cast includes Jane Hayden (sister of exploitation queen Linda), Alison
Elliott (Home Before Midnight) and Jayne Lester, who had to satisfy the British
Board of Film Censors (as it was then) that she was over 16 years old during the
making of the film, due to a scene of sexual assault.

In 2001 Killer's Moon was appraised as a film that "flaunts good taste and
decency with crude bravado, remaining perversely entertaining." Praise indeed
for this mostly forgotten exploitation gem.
