Long Hair of Death (1964), directed by Antonio Margheriti, is a gothic
horror set in medieval Italy. It concerns a woman burned as a witch and
the curse that hangs over the ruling family responsible for her
execution.

British actress Barbara Steele, once described as "the only girl in films
whose eyelids can snarl," plays a dual role: first as Helen Karnstein,
eldest daughter of the murdered woman. She is thrown to her death by Count
Humboldt, the patriarch of the family, and returns reincarnated as the
mysterious stranger Mary to seek revenge on them. Alongside her younger
sister, Lizabeth, who has been forced to marry the cruel Kurt, she plots
that revenge.

Margheriti piles on the dread and rotting corpses to good effect. Steele
shines as always, and Italian actor George Ardisson plays the unhinged
Kurt Humboldt with increasing paranoia. He meets his fiery fate at the
hands of the put-upon Lizabeth, who finally gets revenge for her mother.

Margheriti made Castle of Blood earlier that year, with another standout
performance from Steele. Long Hair of Death pales by comparison, but it's
a notable footnote in the early-60s Italian gothic horror canon, where
Barbara and her extraordinary eyebrows were a regular feature. She would
go on to appear in many horror films across her long career, including
Curse of the Crimson Altar (1968), Shivers (1975) and Piranha (1978).
