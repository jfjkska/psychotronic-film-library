Alice, Sweet Alice (1976) is a Catholic-imbued psychological slasher
directed by Alfred Sole. When Karen (Brooke Shields) is murdered at her
first holy communion, suspicion falls on her precociously cruel older
sister, Alice (Paula Sheppard). An investigating detective, reading
Alice's school report, remarks: "This kid is nuts."

Alice lives in a run-down apartment block and routinely taunts the
landlord, sleazy Mr Alphonso, with jibes of "fatso" and "your apartment
smells of cat piss."

After a brutal knife attack on Alice's Aunt Annie by an assailant wearing
a yellow school coat and mask, the poor girl is carted off to stay in a
mental institution for a psychological assessment. The child psychiatrist
reads out her conclusions on Alice: "Strong indications of a schizoid
personality. Subject capable of extremely violent action."

There are flashes of Psycho-esque violence, and Sole keeps the direction
tight, often using heavy rain to give an even bleaker outlook on the
run-down neighbourhood. The Catholic church features heavily, with family
friend and local priest Father Tom looking inward at what troubles Alice
and her growing resentment towards her family. A reveal thirty minutes
from the end sets the gears in motion on the who and why of people being
strangled, bludgeoned and stabbed, and you're left wondering where it will
go next.

Paula Sheppard is particularly good as Alice and only made one more film
before leaving acting for good. She leaves an indelible mark in her debut,
and Alice, Sweet Alice is a genuine gem in the American slasher canon.
