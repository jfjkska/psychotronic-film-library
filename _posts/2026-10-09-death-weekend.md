Death Weekend (1976) is a Canadian home invasion thriller starring Brenda
Vaccaro, Don Stroud and Chuck Shamata. Directed by William Fruet, it is based
on real incidents, including an experience of Fruet's own.

Diane (Vaccaro) is away for a weekend in rural Ontario with her latest
boyfriend, the wealthy Doctor Harry (Shamata). They encounter a group of
thuggish locals whom they run off the road. Led by Lep (Stroud), the gang
seeks revenge on Diane and Harry and invades the mansion belonging to Harry.
There is a subplot about Harry being a serial womaniser and voyeur, but this
gets lost in the increasing brutality meted out by the gang.

The home invasion builds as the invaders get drunk, and Harry is chased by Lep
and murdered. Diane is raped and stabs her attacker in the neck with a shard
of glass. She is pursued by the other three across the boggy countryside, a
factor that proves fatal to one of them as he closes in on Diane in the
night. Diane shows no mercy in her retribution on her attackers. Vaccaro's
performance is particularly worthy, and Stroud's unhinged malevolence is
unsettling as he taunts his captors.

Death Weekend fell foul of the Video Recordings Act during the video nasty
outrage in the UK and received an outright ban. Critics described it as "a
mindless shocker", "a frighteningly ugly suspense film" and "obscenity in
every sense of the word." It is also known as The House by the Lake, a title
used to capitalise on the notoriety of The Last House on the Left (1972). It
is a precursor to I Spit on Your Grave (1978), and like those more famous
films, its power to shock has not lessened over time.
