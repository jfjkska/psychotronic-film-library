I'm Not Feeling Myself Tonight (1976) was directed by Joseph McGrath and
written by David McGillivray. The cast includes Barry Andrews, James Booth
and Sally Faulkner. Mary Millington has an uncredited role as the girl with
sunglasses. McGillivray has writing credits on [Satan's
Slave]({{ '/reviews/satans-slave/' | relative_url }}),
[Frightmare]({{ '/reviews/frightmare/' | relative_url }}),
[House of Whipcord]({{ '/reviews/house-of-whipcord/' | relative_url }}),
[Schizo]({{ '/reviews/schizo/' | relative_url }}) and [House of Mortal
Sin]({{ '/reviews/house-of-mortal-sin/' | relative_url }}). With a CV like
that, I was expecting something that would stand out from the usual 1970s
British sex comedy. Unfortunately this addition to the genre has little to
recommend it.

The plot, such as it is, concerns John Pigeon (Andrews), a cleaner at a sex
research institute who invents an electronic sonic aphrodisiac machine in an
attempt to seduce his boss's secretary Cheryl (Faulkner). The boss, Nutbrown
(Booth), is after getting his hands on the machine, dubbed AGNES (it is an
acronym, but that really doesn't matter).

The sex research institute consists of patients running around the corridors
naked, a nude aerobics class, and no man is safe from the groin-grabbing tea
lady. Film historian and critic Kim Newman said the film is "an excruciating
comedy with a vaguely offensive plot about a ray gun which turns repressed
Britons into sex maniacs." John ends up head of the institute due to his
invention, and Nutbrown his chauffeur. There is an ongoing gag about no one
remembering Pigeon's surname: he gets called Sparrow, Tit, Mallard, etc. That
really is the level of comedic nuance that McGillivray presents in his
script.

Britain was awash in the 1970s with sexploitation films starring well-known
TV and film actors. Among the cast there are cameos from Brian Murphy and
Graham Stark, who will be familiar to TV audiences. I guess it was a case of
paid work where you could get it. It's better than Derek Ford's dismal
[What's Up Nurse]({{ '/reviews/whats-up-nurse/' | relative_url }}), but far from Pete Walker's [Cool It,
Carol!]({{ '/reviews/cool-it-carol/' | relative_url }}), which certainly
stands out.
