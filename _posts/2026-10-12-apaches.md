Apaches (1977) is a British public information film directed by John
Mackenzie. He would go on to direct the influential British gangster
thriller The Long Good Friday (1980). Apaches was funded by the Central
Office of Information for the Health and Safety Executive. It was shown
in primary schools in the UK, warning children about dangers when
playing outside the home.

The scenario follows six children, with Danny, as Geronimo, our narrator.
They descend on a farm in their imaginary attack as the Apaches of the
title. What follows is a series of deaths by misadventure. These include
falling under the wheels of a tractor trailer, drowning in a slurry pit,
being crushed by a metal gate and, probably the most disturbing, a slow
death from drinking a poisonous substance. The agonised cries of Sharon
in the night, shouting for her "Mummy", will stay with you.

Danny describes a "party" being prepared at home by the adults. It
unfolds that the party is Danny's funeral, with his cousin Michael the
only survivor of the six children. The children were all selected from
the same primary school, and their naturalistic non-acting gives the
film a reality that belies the grim outcome of their fun and games.

The end credits roll with a list of names and ages of children and how
they died in the twelve months leading up to the filming of Apaches.
How any child who watched this in school did not need some counselling
is beyond me. I would have been scarred for life watching this as an
eleven-year-old in 1977.

Apaches is a deep cut of visceral horror that will stay with you a long
time after watching. More public information film horror with The
Finishing Line to follow. You have been warned.
