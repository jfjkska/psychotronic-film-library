Silent Night, Bloody Night (1972) is a proto-slasher directed by Theodore
Gershuny, an early release from Cannon Films.

Mary Woronov sleepwalks through this muddled thriller, set in a town with a
dark past. John Carradine is a deaf publisher who holds a deadly secret
alongside the sheriff, mayor and telephone exchange operator.

Since its release the film has received some reassessment from various film
critics, with praise for its atmosphere and experimental, arthouse qualities,
as well as being noted as a proto-slasher, in particular its use of
point-of-view shots from the killer's perspective. A trope that would become
common after its inclusion in Bob Clark's Black Christmas.

A very odd one to really get to grips with, and certainly not one I would
consider recommending, unless you are a fan of Mary Woronov. And who isn't.
