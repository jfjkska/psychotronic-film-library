The Last Hunter (1980) is a "macaroni combat" film directed by Antonio
Margheriti under the pseudonym Anthony M. Dawson. It freely borrows from the
big-budget films by Cimino and Coppola. Like Apocalypse Now, it was filmed on
the same locations in the Philippines, with interiors shot in Rome. The Last
Hunter punches above its weight and, on the whole, it succeeds in providing a
gritty and gory view of the nihilism of war. Margheriti wanted to make a fun
war movie, but there is no fun contained in its nihilistic portrayal of the
Vietnam War.

A great cast is headed by Kiwi David Warbeck as Captain Morris, on a mission
deep inside Vietcong country. Tisa Farrow (Zombie Flesh Eaters) plays Jane
Foster, a journalist who becomes inextricably linked to Captain Morris's
mission. Tony King plays George "Wash" Washington, the lone survivor of the
team ordered to support Morris in his plan. Margit Evelyn Newton (Zombie
Creeping Flesh) is Carol, a figure from Morris's past who haunts his dreams.
Carol is providing the anti-war bulletins for the Vietcong, urging the GIs to
lay down their weapons and return to their girlfriends. Morris's mission is to
destroy the transmitter that sends out the broadcasts.

The Last Hunter is more than an exploitation film riding on the coat-tails of
the US studio pictures. It really sets its bar high and mostly succeeds. The
cinematography by Riccardo Pallottini provides a big-budget view of its
locations, and Margheriti directs the action with a grim determination to show
every detail of its bloody narrative.

I first watched this film on a worn VHS in the early eighties from my local
video rental store and had forgotten what a great film it is. It was seized
under the UK Obscene Publications Act during the video nasty panic. Highly
recommended for anyone wanting to see how the Italians viewed this dark chapter
in American history.
