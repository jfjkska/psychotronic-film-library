More Eurosleaze, and ramp it up to ten with the godfather of the genre, Jess
Franco. Apparently filmed less than a month after Vampyros Lesbos, his muse
Soledad Miranda plays the vengeful wife of a doctor who kills himself. She
goes after the four board members, one of whom is Franco himself, who barred
her husband from practising after they rejected his research on human embryos.

It's all here in spadefuls: full frontal nudity, bloodletting, lesbian sex,
necromancy. Franco knows how to linger and he holds on to frames much longer
than really needed, but that is his oeuvre. Miranda is pretty good and
certainly more coherent than in her role in The Bare Breasted Countess.

The soundtrack is pure kraut lounge gold, with its jaunty horns and psych
sitar. It fills in the gaps in the narrative and works well for the most part.

Franco really was the most hardworking director in the 70s, and this was his
third film in 1971. He was admired by Christopher Lee, who made two Fu Manchu
films with him and said that Franco really understood the character of Dracula
when he cast him in the role, with full moustache, in 1970.

Certainly worth checking out and, although Franco's work can be indifferent,
this one was very nearly on target.
