Italian psychosexual thriller The Laughing Woman (1969) is a really
interesting slice of genre filmmaking.

A young press agent, Maria, played by Dagmar Lassander, is held captive by the
director of a philanthropic organisation, who subjects her to an array of
humiliating tortures to satisfy his sadistic fantasies. She employs her own
mind games to turn this game of cat and mouse on its head.

A decidedly groovy set design and a great soundtrack by Stelvio Cipriani,
which blends psychedelia, jazz and funk to great effect, all contribute to a
darkly twisted tale of sexual one-upmanship.
