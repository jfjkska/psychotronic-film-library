Keep It Up, Jack (1974) is another entry from the incorrigible Derek Ford, he
who brought What's Up Nurse!, Groupie Girl and many other sexploitation
favourites to the cinemas of the early 1970s. Mark Jones plays the put-upon
Jack, a quick-change artist who finds himself the recipient of a redundant
brothel from his dead Auntie. While perusing the "house of ill repute" he
chances upon Virginia, played by the prolific sexploitation star Sue Longhurst.
She has never met Auntie, so Jack hatches a plan to impersonate his dead
relative and bring the business back with the help of Virginia and her
contacts of girls and punters.

What ensues is a catalogue of saucy encounters involving whipped cream, occult
whipping and a champagne-soaked threesome. The film is punctuated with the
a cappella harmony "Keep It Up, Jack", just to remind the viewer that Jack has
more on his plate than he can really deal with. Jack falls in love with new
girl Fleur, played by the cute Maggi Burton, and a plot is made to kill off the
character of Auntie so Jack can finally get his girl.

As 70s sexploitation goes it has the usual bedroom farce, nudity and some racy
scenes that are a staple of Derek Ford's oeuvre. The Guardian's Derek Malcolm
wrote: "you might think that with a modicum of talent, the film might be
mildly erotic or passably funny. But believe me, it's not." It has gained a
more favourable cult retro-reputation since that scathing review, and I think
it is a must-see for fans of British sex comedies.
