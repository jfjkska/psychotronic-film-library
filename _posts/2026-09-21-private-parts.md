Private Parts (1972) is Paul Bartel's feature debut. It is part art house
exploration of gender expectations and part exploitation trash, with women who
are men and men who are women.

All set behind a facade of art deco exterior, this houses a "respectable
hotel" of sadomasochism imagery, voyeurism, and where unwanted snooping leads
to decapitation. Bartel's direction leads us through the dank corridors and
the secrets of its residents through the eyes of Cheryl, a runaway looking to
explore her own sexual awakening.

The soundtrack dominates the mood; a more considered approach to the use of
music to build the tension would have been an improvement. Bartel has
uncredited writing on the script he developed, and also pops up in a brief
cameo as a park dweller disturbed by Cheryl in a private moment.

Bartel would go on to make more successful films, but this is an interesting
start to his feature film career, with odd shifts between the intellectual and
grindhouse tropes.
